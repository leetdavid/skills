# Decision meeting

An executive meeting exists to get a decision. Prepare it so the decider has seen everything before they walk in, hears the recommendation in the first sentence, and leaves having said yes, no, or by when they will decide.

## Workflow

Draft the whole kit from what you already know before asking anything. Label inferred content **Proposed** and missing facts **To confirm**. Then ask up to three questions per round, choosing the ones whose answers would change the ask, the D, or the reasons. Use only numbers the user supplied or that appear in sources in context, and mark every other figure **To confirm**.

1. **Pin the ask.** Write the decision as one sentence the D can answer yes or no, carrying the user's recommendation. "Approve $400K to move support to Zendesk by Q2" is an ask. "Discuss support tooling" is an agenda, so turn it into an ask.
2. **Find the D.** Name the one person who makes the call (the D in Bain's RAPID) and list who only gives input. When the person in the meeting gives input only, the goal becomes getting them to recommend the decision to the D, or getting the D into the room. Say which. Record what the D is measured on, such as targets, KPIs, and board commitments. The reasons in step 4 hang on these.
3. **Pre-wire.** Everyone who can block hears the recommendation and its numbers privately before the meeting, so the meeting holds no surprises. Draft the questions the user takes to their champion (the insider backing the recommendation), starting with "What would need to be true for you to say yes?" and "Who makes the final call, and who else needs to be on board?" Give each other blocker their own pre-wire questions. Pair every known or likely objection with the part of the one page that answers it. An objection the page can't answer is a gap to raise with the user now.
4. **Write the one page.** Use the template below. It goes out 48 hours before the meeting so the D reads it first and the meeting is spent deciding. Write it answer first, with the recommendation as the opening sentence. Give three reasons at most, each tied to something the D is measured on. Every number passes the so-what test.
5. **Script the open and close.** The user's first sentence in the room is the recommendation, ahead of any agenda or background. The close asks for the decision outright. Write a fallback for when the D defers: ask what they would need to see, and fix a date for the decision. The meeting ends with a yes, a no, or a dated next step owned by a named person.

Finish when the ask is one yes-or-no sentence, the D is named or flagged **To confirm**, every blocker has pre-wire questions, the page fits on one page and opens with the recommendation, every number passes the so-what test, and the open and close are scripted.

## The so-what test

Read each number as the D would and ask "so what?" It passes when the same sentence says what the number does to something the D is measured on.

- Fails: "Ticket volume grew 38% year over year."
- Passes: "Ticket volume grew 38%, which puts the Q3 CSAT target out of reach without new tooling."

Rewrite a number that fails, or cut it.

## One-page template

```markdown
# <The ask>

**Recommendation:** <what to do, by when, at what cost, in one sentence>

**Why**
1. <reason tied to a metric the D owns, with its number and so-what>
2. <...>
3. <...>

**Risks**
- <objection raised in pre-wiring>: <how the plan handles it>

**Decision needed:** <yes or no on the ask> by <date>
```

## Output

Return the kit under these headings: The ask, The D, Pre-wire, One page, Open and close, then To confirm when gaps remain. When the user asks for one piece, such as only the one page, return that piece and apply the rules that govern it.

## Reviewing an existing pitch

When the user brings a deck, memo, or talk track, treat it as a draft of the kit. Check it against every rule in the workflow and the so-what test, quote each line that breaks one, then rewrite it. Replace a deck with a one page.
